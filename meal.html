Fuel first — growth-protective recomposition

At 5'8", 155 lb, and still growing, performance and normal development come before an aggressive body-fat target. Start with regular meals and adjust with a parent, pediatrician, or sports dietitian.

  • Protein: 110–135g/day as a starting range, spread across 4–5 feedings — poultry, fish, eggs, yogurt, milk, tofu, beans, or lean meat
  • Carbs: include rice, potatoes, oats, fruit, pasta, bread, or beans at meals; volleyball and jumping need glycogen
  • Plate anchor: about ½ produce, ¼ protein, ¼ carbohydrate, plus a calcium-rich food and a little unsaturated fat
  • No crash cut: do not fast, skip post-practice food, use fat burners, or cut carbohydrates to chase a lower scale weight
  • Body composition: aim first for stable energy, stronger lifts, a better jump, and a stable waist; gradual recomposition is safer than rapid weight loss

Practice fuel — Friday + Sunday

  • 2–3 hours before: a normal meal such as chicken/tofu + rice or pasta + fruit and vegetables
  • 30–60 minutes before: banana, applesauce, cereal bar, pretzels, toast, or yogurt if hungry
  • During 2½-hour practice: sip water regularly; a sports drink can be useful for a sweaty session of this length — skip energy drinks
  • After Friday practice: at about 10:20 PM, have a recovery snack such as chocolate milk, yogurt + cereal, or a turkey/bean sandwich, then sleep
  • After Sunday practice: eat dinner soon after getting home at about 6:35 PM; include both carbohydrate and protein

Simple school-day template

  • Breakfast: oats with milk or fortified soy milk, berries, and eggs or yogurt
  • School snack: fruit + Greek yogurt, cheese, nuts, or a peanut-butter sandwich
  • Lunch: chicken, fish, tofu, beans, or turkey with rice/potatoes, vegetables, and fruit
  • Pre-training: sandwich or leftovers plus fruit; add pretzels or a granola bar before practice
  • Dinner: salmon/chicken/tofu, pasta/rice/potatoes, vegetables, and milk or fortified alternative
  • Before bed: optional yogurt, cereal with milk, fruit, or toast if hungry — recovery does not stop at 8 PM

Hydration + body-composition feedback

  • Daily: carry a bottle and drink with meals; urine should generally be pale yellow, not clear all day
  • Practice: drink small amounts often and replace a salty meal/snack afterward when sweating heavily
  • Track weekly: note jump quality, lifting progress, practice energy, sleep, hunger, and waist/clothing fit — not just scale weight
  • Red flags: persistent fatigue, irritability, dizziness, frequent illness, missed periods in teammates, poor sleep, or falling performance means tell an adult and increase support
  • Medical check: if prior LDL or cholesterol results are still abnormal, review them with a clinician; favor oats, beans, fruit, vegetables, fish, nuts, and olive oil rather than banning whole food groups

Growth-supporting nutrients — food first

3 servingsCalcium-rich foods 4–5Protein feedings DailyFruit + vegetables 1–2x/weekFatty fish OftenOats, beans, whole grains

Food is the default. Do not start high-dose vitamins, minerals, creatine, or other supplements without a parent/guardian and clinician or sports dietitian. No meal plan can guarantee extra height; adequate energy, nutrient variety, sleep, and normal puberty support the growth you are genetically able to achieve.
