Training Plan

Push · pull · legs · upper · lower · volleyball-ready recovery

Weekly program — five lifting days, two recovery days

Built around a 16-year-old, 5'8\", 155 lb volleyball athlete. If a team practice or tournament lands on a lifting day, practice wins: move or reduce the lift rather than stacking fatigue.

MONDay 1 · PUSH — chest, shoulders, triceps
  • 3 x 6-10Barbell or DB Bench Press
  • 3 x 8-12Incline DB Press
  • 3 x 6-10Seated DB Shoulder Press
  • 2 x 10-15Cable or DB Lateral Raise
  • 2 x 8-12Cable Pressdown
  • 2 x 10-15Overhead Cable Triceps Extension

Warm up with 2–3 lighter ramp-up sets. Keep 2–3 reps in reserve; no forced reps.

TUEDay 2 · PULL — back, rear delts, biceps
  • 3 x 6-10Pull-up or Neutral-Grip Lat Pulldown
  • 3 x 8-12Chest-Supported Row
  • 2 x 10-15Single-Arm Cable Row
  • 2 x 12-15Face Pull or Reverse Pec-Deck
  • 2 x 8-12Incline DB Curl
  • 2 x 10-15Hammer Curl

Use a full controlled stretch and stop before swinging. Add load only when every rep is clean.

WEDDay 3 · LEGS — strength + jump quality
  • 3 x 5-8Safety-Bar Squat, Back Squat, or Leg Press
  • 3 x 6-10Romanian Deadlift
  • 2 x 8-12/legReverse Lunge
  • 2 x 8-12Seated or Lying Hamstring Curl
  • 2 x 10-15Standing Calf Raise
  • 3 x 3Countermovement Jump — full reset

Jumps come first after the warm-up. Stop at the first clear drop in height or landing quality; never chase exhausted contacts.

THUDay 4 · REST — recover, do not “make up” work
  • 20-30 minOptional easy walk or bike at conversational pace
  • 10 minEasy ankle, hip, shoulder, and T-spine mobility
  • priorityEat normally, hydrate, and protect 8–10 hours of sleep
  • skipNo lifting, max jumps, sprints, or failure circuits

A rest day is productive. Soreness, poor sleep, or a hard practice means keeping this fully easy.

FRIDay 5 · UPPER — chest, back, shoulders
  • 3 x 6-10DB Bench Press
  • 3 x 6-10Pull-up or Lat Pulldown
  • 3 x 8-12Seated Cable Row
  • 2 x 8-12Landmine Press or Incline DB Press
  • 2 x 12-15Lateral Raise
  • 2 x 12-15Reverse Pec-Deck

This is moderate volume, not a second max-out. If volleyball is today, cut each exercise to 2 sets or skip the lift.

SATDay 6 · LOWER — hinge, single-leg, trunk
  • 3 x 5-8Trap-Bar Deadlift or Hip Thrust
  • 3 x 8-12Front-Foot-Elevated Split Squat
  • 2 x 8-12Leg Press or Goblet Squat
  • 2 x 10-15Hamstring Curl
  • 2 x 10-15Seated Calf Raise
  • 2 x 30-45 secPallof Press or Side Plank

Keep one or two good reps available on every lower-body set. Avoid this session before a tournament or high-volume practice.

SUNDay 7 · REST — reset for the next week
  • optionalEasy walk, mobility, or light ball handling
  • 10 minLow-intensity hips, calves, ankles, and shoulders
  • priorityPrepare meals, review the training log, and sleep
  • adjustIf soreness persists, take another rest day instead of forcing Day 1

Return to the split when you can move well and perform the warm-up without pain.

Evidence-based rules

  • Effort: begin around 2–3 reps in reserve; failure is not required for strength or muscle, especially when technique and recovery matter.
  • Volume: start with the listed 2–3 work sets. Add one set only after 2–3 weeks of good recovery and stable volleyball performance.
  • Progression: when every set reaches the top of its rep range with clean form, add the smallest available load next time.
  • Rest: take 2–3 minutes on compound lifts and 60–120 seconds on isolation work; longer is fine when needed for quality.
  • Deload: every 4–6 hard weeks, or sooner if sleep, performance, motivation, or joint comfort drops: reduce sets by about one-third to one-half.

Volleyball load controls

  • Jump first: keep Day 3 jumps to 6–10 high-quality contacts after warming up; practices and matches already count.
  • Hard-day spacing: do not place Day 3 or Day 6 legs immediately before a tournament or high-volume practice.
  • Practice swap: a hard practice replaces the closest lifting session; do not “make up” missed work by doubling.
  • Red flags: stop for sharp pain, swelling, limping, chest pain, dizziness, or persistent joint pain and tell an adult/clinician.
  • Supervision: a qualified coach should teach squat, hinge, press, and pull technique before increasing load.

Why this split · sources

This plan uses a moderate 5-day split because the requested schedule gives each major muscle group two weekly exposures while separating the two lower-body days. The exercise menu prioritizes stable multi-joint patterns, manageable volume, and power work while fresh—not bodybuilding failure or maximal testing. Evidence is strongest for resistance-training principles in adults, so the youth-specific safety and supervision guidance takes priority.

  • NSCA youth resistance-training position statement — supervised, progressive training is appropriate for youth; technique and gradual progression come first.
  • Schoenfeld et al., 2019 frequency meta-analysis — when weekly volume is matched, frequency itself is less important; this split distributes volume to keep sessions manageable.
  • Grgic et al., 2022 failure meta-analysis — training to momentary failure is not necessary for strength or hypertrophy.
  • Hernandez-Martinez et al., 2023 plyometric-frequency study — supports planned, quality plyometric work rather than unstructured high-volume jumping.
